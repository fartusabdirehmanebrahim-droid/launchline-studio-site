DV Lottery photo with a hijab or religious head covering

Many people entering the DV Lottery ask the same question: can I wear my hijab in the photo? The short answer is yes. U.S. visa and DV photos allow a head covering that you wear for religious reasons, as long as your full face is clearly visible. This guide explains what that means in practice, the mistakes that cause problems, and how to take a good photo at home.

What the official rules say

The Department of State's photo rules on travel.state.gov say that hats and head coverings are not allowed, except head coverings worn for religious purposes. When you wear one:

  • Your full face must be visible, from the bottom of the chin to the top of the forehead, and from one side of the face to the other.
  • The head covering must not cast shadows on your face.

This applies to the hijab, the turban, the kippah and other religious coverings worn daily. A fashion hat, cap, hood or scarf worn only for style is not allowed.

The rule is about the face, so a covering that hides part of the face, such as a niqab or a veil over the mouth, cannot be worn for the photo. The face must be uncovered for the picture. If you prefer, a female family member can take the photo at home in private.

Common mistakes with a hijab in the photo

  1. Fabric over the forehead or chin. Pull the hijab back slightly so the forehead, eyebrows and chin line are all visible. The edges of the face should not be hidden.
  2. Shadows on the face. A hijab that comes forward like a hood throws a shadow onto the forehead and cheeks. Face a window and keep the fabric close to the head.
  3. A white hijab on a white background. It is not forbidden, but a white or cream hijab can blend into the wall and make the outline of the head unclear. A medium or darker plain colour usually looks clearer.
  4. Busy patterns and shiny pins. A plain, matte fabric photographs best. Large shiny brooches or jewellery near the face can cause glare.
  5. Using an old photo. The photo must be taken within the last 6 months, even if you look the same.
  6. Different appearance at the interview. If you are selected, it helps if you look like your photo. Wearing your head covering the same way as in daily life avoids confusion.

How to take the photo at home: step by step

  1. Choose a plain white wall and stand about half a metre in front of it so your shadow falls behind you, not on the wall.
  2. Face a window in daylight. Light from the front is even and soft. Avoid the phone flash and overhead lamps, which cause shadows under the hijab edge.
  3. Arrange the hijab so the whole face is open: forehead, eyebrows, both cheeks and chin. Smooth any loose fabric.
  4. Ask someone to take the picture from 1–1.5 metres away, with the phone at your eye level. Selfies distort the face.
  5. Look straight at the camera with a neutral expression, mouth closed and both eyes open. Remove glasses.
  6. Take several photos and choose the sharpest one, with no shadows and the face straight.

Final checklist before you upload

  • Head covering is worn for religious reasons, not fashion
  • Full face visible: forehead, eyebrows, cheeks and chin
  • No shadows from the fabric on the face
  • Plain white or off-white background
  • No glasses, no headphones, no filters or editing
  • Head (including the covering) fills 50–69% of the height
  • 600×600 JPEG, 240 KB or smaller
  • Taken in the last 6 months
