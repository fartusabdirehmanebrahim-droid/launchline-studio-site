How to reduce a DV Lottery photo to 240 KB

A photo from a phone is usually 2–6 MB and a tall rectangle. The DV Lottery and U.S. visa systems need a small square JPEG of 240 KB or less. If your file is too big, the upload will not work. This guide explains how to make the file smaller the right way, so your face stays sharp and the photo still meets the rules.

The file rules you are aiming for

The official requirements are on travel.state.gov. For the digital file:

RuleRequirement
FormatJPEG (.jpg)
File size240 KB or smaller
DimensionsSquare, at least 600×600 pixels and at most 1200×1200 pixels
ColourIn colour (24 bits per pixel), not black and white
Head sizeChin to top of head is 50–69% of the image height
RecentTaken within the last 6 months

Making the file small is not the only goal. The photo must also stay unaltered: you can crop and compress it, but you must not edit your face, remove shadows by painting, change the background with an app, or use filters.

Why the size is the easy part

Most of a phone photo's size comes from its pixel count. A 4000×3000 photo has 12 million pixels. A 600×600 photo has only 360,000, which is about 33 times fewer. So the most important step is not "compressing" the photo, it is cropping and resizing it to 600×600. At 600×600, a JPEG saved at normal quality is usually already under the limit.

Step by step: reduce the photo correctly

  1. Start from the original file. Use the photo straight from the camera, not a copy sent through WhatsApp or Facebook. Those apps compress the picture and lower the quality before you even start.
  2. Crop to a square first. Crop around your head and shoulders so the image is square. Place your head in the centre, with your eyes a little above the middle. Your head (chin to top of hair) should fill 50–69% of the height.
  3. Resize to 600×600 pixels. In any photo editor, choose "resize" or "image size" and set width and height to 600 pixels. Keep the aspect ratio locked so the face is not stretched.
  4. Save as JPEG. Choose "Save as" or "Export" and pick JPEG. Do not save as PNG, HEIC or WEBP; these are not accepted.
  5. Set the quality. If the editor shows a quality slider, start at around 85–90%. Check the file size. If it is still over 240 KB, lower the quality a little at a time (for example to 80%) until it fits.
  6. Check the result. Open the saved file and zoom in. Your eyes, hair edge and face should look clear, with no blocky squares or colour bands. If it looks blocky, the quality is too low.
  7. Confirm the size. On a computer, right-click the file and choose "Properties" (Windows) or "Get Info" (Mac). On a phone, use the file manager's details view. It must say 240 KB or less.

Mistakes that make the photo fail

  • Shrinking without cropping. If you only resize a tall photo, it becomes squashed or the head becomes far too small.
  • Compressing too much. Setting quality very low (like 20–30%) makes the file tiny but blurry and blocky. A photo that is not clear can be rejected.
  • Using a "photo compressor" website that changes the size. Some tools reduce the KB but also change the pixels to an odd size like 480×480. Always check the dimensions after.
  • Screenshots. Taking a screenshot of your photo adds a border or changes the size and quality. Save the file properly instead.
  • Renaming a file to .jpg. Changing "photo.png" to "photo.jpg" does not make it a JPEG. You must export it in JPEG format.
  • Background or face edits. Apps that "beautify" or replace the background alter the photo, which is not allowed.

Is a smaller file "better"?

No. Anything up to 240 KB is fine. A 200 KB file with clear details is better than a 30 KB file that looks soft. Aim for the best quality that still fits under the limit. If you use a larger size such as 1200×1200, you will need more compression to stay under 240 KB, so 600×600 is usually the simplest choice.

Checklist before you upload

  • Started from the original camera file, not a WhatsApp copy
  • Cropped to a square with the head at 50–69% of the height
  • Exactly 600×600 pixels (or square, up to 1200×1200)
  • Saved as a real JPEG file
  • 240 KB or smaller
  • Sharp when zoomed in, no blocky areas
  • In colour, no filters, no face or background edits
  • Taken in the last 6 months
