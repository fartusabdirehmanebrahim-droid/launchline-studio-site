Does a small cleaning business need a website?

Many cleaning businesses start with a phone number, a WhatsApp status and a few happy customers who tell their friends. That works, and for some owners it is enough. But when a new customer needs a cleaner, they often search on Google first: "house cleaning near me", "office cleaning in [city]", "move-out cleaning". If your business has no website, you are usually not in those results, and the customer calls someone else. Here is when a website is worth it and what it should include.

The short answer

You do not need a website to clean well. You need one if you want customers who do not already know you. A website lets strangers find you, check that you are real, see what you offer and contact you, all without you having to answer the same questions on the phone again and again.

Social media is still useful for showing recent jobs. The best setup is usually both: a website as your permanent "shop front", and social posts that link back to it.

Signs a website would help your cleaning business

  • Most new jobs come from people you already know, and you want to grow beyond that circle.
  • You want office, commercial or end-of-tenancy contracts, where managers expect to see a website before they call.
  • You answer the same questions every day: price, areas, what is included, whether you bring supplies.
  • A competitor appears on Google for your city and you do not.
  • You want to look as professional as the quality of your work.

What a cleaning business website should include

You do not need many pages. One well-made page is often enough for a small cleaning business. Make sure it has:

  1. A clear headline. Say exactly what you do and where, for example "Home and office cleaning in Hargeisa". This helps both customers and Google.
  2. Your services. A short list: regular home cleaning, deep cleaning, move-in/move-out, office cleaning, carpets, windows. Only list what you really do.
  3. Service areas. The neighbourhoods or cities you cover.
  4. How pricing works. You do not have to publish every price, but explain how you quote: per visit, per room or per hour, and whether supplies are included.
  5. Easy contact. A tap-to-call button, a WhatsApp link and a short quote form. Most visitors will be on a phone.
  6. Real photos. Photos of your team and your own work (with the client's permission). Avoid stock photos that look nothing like your business.
  7. Trust details. Your business name, how long you have worked, opening hours, and whether you are insured or registered, if that is true. Only add customer reviews that real customers actually gave you.
  8. Fast loading. A page that takes a long time to open on mobile data loses visitors. Keep it simple and light.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • No city name anywhere. Google cannot show you for "cleaning in [your city]" if the city is not on the page.
  • Contact details hidden at the bottom. Put the call or WhatsApp button near the top and repeat it at the end.
  • Invented reviews or numbers. Fake testimonials damage trust when customers notice, and they can break advertising rules. Use real feedback or none.
  • A site that never changes. Old prices or a disconnected number make people think you have closed. Update it when things change.

How much should it cost?

Prices vary a lot. A simple landing page does not need a large budget or an expensive monthly plan. The ongoing costs are usually a domain name (often around $10–15 a year) and, if you want someone else to make changes for you, an optional maintenance fee. Before you pay anyone, ask to see a draft, ask what happens if you want changes, and ask who owns the domain. The domain should be registered in your name.
