DV Lottery photo for a baby or child

If you enter the DV Lottery and you have children, each child needs their own photo. Babies and small children are the hardest photos to get right: they move, they close their eyes, and parents often end up in the picture. This guide explains the rules for children's photos and gives practical steps for taking one at home with a phone.

Which children need a photo?

According to the official DV instructions, you must list your spouse and all of your unmarried children under 21 on your entry, with a separate photo for each person. This includes children who do not live with you and children who do not plan to move to the United States. Leaving out an eligible child, or using the wrong person's photo, can disqualify the whole entry. Read the current official instructions on travel.state.gov before you enter.

Group or family photos are not accepted. Every person gets their own picture showing only them.

The rules are the same as for adults

A child's DV photo follows the same technical rules as an adult photo:

SizeSquare, 600×600 pixels (up to 1200×1200)

The Department of State's photo page adds two points for babies and toddlers: no other person should be in the photo, and the child should be looking at the camera with eyes open.

Babies grow and change fast, so a photo from when your child was younger may no longer match the 6-month rule or how they look now. Always take a new one.

How to photograph a baby (0–12 months)

  1. Lay the baby on a plain white sheet. Spread a clean white or off-white sheet, blanket or towel on a bed or the floor and lay the baby on their back. This is often easier than holding them up.
  2. Use soft, even light. Daylight from a window works well. Avoid the phone flash and avoid standing so that your own shadow falls on the baby.
  3. Hold the phone directly above. Keep the phone parallel to the baby's face, about an arm's length away, so the face is not distorted. Leave space around the head for cropping.
  4. Get their attention. A second person can stand behind you making a sound or holding a toy just above the phone so the baby looks into the camera. The helper and toy must stay out of the shot.
  5. Take many shots. Use burst mode and pick the one where the eyes are open, the face is straight and the mouth is neutral.
  6. Remove dummies and toys. No pacifier, bottle, toy or hat in the photo.

How to photograph a toddler or older child

  1. Use a car seat or chair against a white wall for toddlers who will not stay still. Cover the car seat with a white sheet so only white shows behind the head.
  2. Older children can stand about half a metre in front of a plain white wall, facing a window, so shadows don't fall behind them.
  3. Keep the hair off the face so the full face is visible. Religious head coverings worn daily are allowed if the full face is visible.
  4. Neutral expression, eyes open, looking straight at the camera. A natural, closed-mouth face is best.
  5. No hands in the picture. If a parent supports the child, their hands, arms and body must not appear anywhere in the final photo.

Before you upload: final checklist

  • Only the child is in the photo, no parent, hands or toys
  • Eyes open and looking at the camera
  • Plain white or off-white background, no strong shadows
  • Head fills 50–69% of the height and is centred
  • 600×600 JPEG, 240 KB or smaller
  • Taken in the last 6 months and not edited with filters
  • One separate photo for each child, matched to the right name on the form
