How to take a DV Lottery or US visa photo with your phone

You do not need a photo studio to make a good DV Lottery or U.S. visa photo. A modern phone camera is sharp enough. What matters is the background, the light, the distance and the final file. This guide walks you through each step so you can take the photo at home in about ten minutes.

What the photo must meet

The official rules are on travel.state.gov. The main points for a digital photo are:

RuleRequirement
SizeSquare, 600×600 pixels (up to 1200×1200)
FileJPEG, 240 KB or smaller
Head sizeChin to top of head fills 50–69% of the photo height
BackgroundPlain white or off-white, no shadows or patterns
FaceFacing the camera, neutral expression, both eyes open
ColourIn colour, not black and white, with no filters
RecentTaken within the last 6 months
GlassesNot allowed (rare medical exceptions)

Your phone will not produce this file by itself. It takes a tall rectangle of several megabytes. You take a good picture first, then crop and compress it (see the last step).

Step by step

  1. Prepare the background. Use a plain white or off-white wall with no pictures, switches or marks behind your head. If your walls are coloured, hang a smooth white sheet and pull it tight so there are no folds.
  2. Stand away from the wall. Stand about half a metre in front of it. This stops your shadow from falling on the background.
  3. Get the light right. Face a window during the day so soft light falls evenly on your face. Do not stand with the window behind you, and turn off the flash. Overhead lamps make shadows under the eyes and nose.
  4. Get ready. Remove glasses, headphones and hats. A head covering worn for religious reasons is allowed if your full face is visible (see our hijab guide). Move hair away from your eyes.
  5. Set the distance. The helper stands about 1 to 1.5 metres away, with the phone at the height of your eyes and held straight, not tilted. Avoid selfies at arm's length: the camera is too close and makes the nose look bigger and the face uneven.
  6. Frame loosely. Leave space around your head and shoulders. Do not zoom in close; it is better to crop later than to cut off the top of your head or chin.
  7. Pose. Look straight into the camera, keep your shoulders square, mouth closed and both eyes open. Use a neutral expression or a very light natural smile.
  8. Take several shots. Tap on your face to focus, then take five or six photos. Choose the sharpest one with no shadows and your head straight.

Phone settings to check

  • Turn off beauty mode, portrait mode and filters. These change your face or blur the background. The photo must not be digitally altered.
  • Use the normal photo mode at 1× zoom.
  • Do not use a black-and-white mode; the photo must be in colour.
  • Send the original file. Apps like WhatsApp compress photos heavily. If someone else took the picture, ask them to send it as a "document" or file so the quality is kept.

Common phone photo mistakes

  1. Shadow on the wall behind the head, from standing too close to it
  2. One side of the face darker than the other, from light coming from the side
  3. Head too small in the frame, or the top of the head cut off
  4. Blurry picture from moving or low light at night
  5. A grey or yellow background from indoor bulbs
  6. Using an old photo instead of taking a new one

Final step: make the 600×600 file

Once you have a good picture, it must be cropped to a square with your head at the right size and saved as a JPEG of 240 KB or less. It cannot guarantee acceptance, because only the Department of State decides, but it helps you avoid the common technical mistakes.

Checklist before you upload

  • Plain white or off-white background, no shadows
  • Even light on the face, no flash
  • Taken by another person from 1–1.5 metres, at eye level
  • No glasses, filters, beauty mode or editing
  • Facing the camera, neutral expression, eyes open
  • Head fills 50–69% of the height
  • 600×600 JPEG, 240 KB or smaller
  • Taken in the last 6 months
